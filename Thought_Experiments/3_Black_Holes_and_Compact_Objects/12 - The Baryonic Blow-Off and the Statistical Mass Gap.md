## Thought Experiment 12: The Baryonic Blow-Off and the Statistical Mass Gap

**The Scenario:**
Following a core-collapse supernova, astronomical observations reveal a stark "Lower Mass Gap." The heaviest known neutron stars generally max out around 2.2 to 2.5 $M_\odot$, while the lightest known black holes begin around 5 $M_\odot$. The 3-to-4 $M_\odot$ weight class is statistically barren. Standard models struggle to explain this void. Relying on continuous mathematical limits (like the TOV limit) that inevitably lead to unphysical singularities, mainstream physics assumes that matter entering this gap is physically crushed out of existence. The Finite Spatial Medium (FSM) rejects this mathematical cliff. Because the continuous equations that produce the singularity are fundamentally broken, their associated breaking points are equally invalid. The FSM models the mass gap not as a structural prohibition of the spatial substrate, but strictly as the statistical artifact of bifurcated baryonic blow-off dynamics and the extreme rarity of secondary coalescences.

**The Principal Architect's Core Mechanics Insight:**
*"I think structurally when you have a supernova happen, it's either big enough to form a black hole or it blows too much off and forms a neutron star. I don't think it has anything to do with any yield strength in the spatial network or anything like that.  If it doesn't have enough to do that, there's no in-between stage. It blows off too much and you end up with this roughly 2.2 mass neutron star. Now, that doesn't mean there can't be masses in between that... but the rarity of those objects is because they have to actually collide with something. It's not due to any inherent structural thing."*

**Substrate Architecture & The Bifurcated Collapse:** 
Under the framework's discrete mechanics, the spatial substrate responds to mass via smooth, non-linear localized volumetric node compression ($\text{ש}_V$). The spatial network possesses no arbitrary "yield strength" that chemically or structurally forbids a 3.5 $M_\odot$ mass anchor. The gap is driven by stellar mechanics, not spatial limitations:
- **The Optical Curtain (The FSM Horizon):** Because the FSM dictates that the core structurally clamps against a physical volumetric floor rather than collapsing into a zero-volume singularity, a 5 $M_\odot$ "black hole" is simply a denser physical core. It is massive enough to steepen the localized spatial compression gradient ($\text{ש}_V$) until it causes total internal refraction of light (the event horizon). Structurally, the core of a 5 $M_\odot$ black hole may functionally be a massive neutron star hidden behind an optical threshold, sitting comfortably on the intermediate Structural Density Ladder.
- **The Binary Blow-Off Extremes:** During a supernova, the core undergoes a violent elastomechanical ricochet. This massive kinetic rebound naturally bifurcates into two extremes: either the progenitor is so overwhelmingly massive that the core immediately clamps the substrate down past the optical horizon threshold, or the violent ricochet successfully blasts the vast majority of the envelope away. The exact final mass of the surviving neutron star is not fixed by an arbitrary mathematical cliff; it dynamically spans from roughly 1.2 to over 2.5 $M_\odot$ because the core's capacity to withstand collapse is strictly governed by its localized pre-existing spin rate (centrifugal shear support, $\text{ש}_S$) and the exact geometric asymmetry of the baryonic expulsion. There is no stable middle ground where a dying star cleanly halts its explosive ejection halfway.
- **The Merger Exception:** Because 3-to-4 $M_\odot$ objects are not physically forbidden by the spatial substrate, they can and do exist—but they must be built dynamically rather than born directly. This mass accumulation can occur through various secondary events. For example, a 4 $M_\odot$ compact object can form if two 2 $M_\odot$ neutron stars collide and merge. Alternatively, a 2.2 $M_\odot$ neutron star could collide with and consume a standard main-sequence star (like our Sun), swallowing enough baryonic mass to push deep into the statistical void. However, because these secondary macroscopic collisions are orders of magnitude less frequent than standard core-collapse supernovae, the resulting gap-mass objects are statistical outliers, leaving an apparent "gap" in standard stellar population surveys.

If the Lower Mass Gap is a statistical artifact of stellar ejection and secondary merging rather than a fundamental physical breakdown, observational catalogs must reflect the presence of gap-mass objects exactly where secondary accretion or collisions occur.
- **Gravitational Wave Ringdown Ratios:** Current ground-based observatories (LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA) have begun detecting the inspiral phase of rare mergers involving objects in the mass gap (e.g., the 2.6 $M_\odot$ secondary object in GW190814). Standard astrophysics is highly divided on whether to classify these as the heaviest neutron stars or the lightest black holes. Because LIGO's high-frequency limitations prevent it from capturing the delayed core ringdown, the FSM provides a decisive future diagnostic via next-generation broadband and space-based interferometers (such as LISA). Finding no structural phase-transition signature between the two masses verifies that black holes and neutron stars occupy the exact same continuous physical spectrum on the structural density ladder.
- **X-Ray Accretion "Fill-In":** The framework predicts that the 3-to-4 $M_\odot$ bracket will gradually populate not through new supernovae, but through High-Mass X-Ray Binaries (HMXBs). High-precision orbital telemetry from X-ray observatories will eventually confirm isolated cases of compact objects that began as standard neutron stars but slowly siphoned enough mass from a companion over billions of years to creep upward into the 3.5 $M_\odot$ range. Whether this accreted mass eventually triggers the Optical Curtain (rendering it a low-mass "black hole" to standard observers) or remains visible, the telemetry will prove the spatial substrate perfectly accommodates mass anchors inside the statistical gap.
