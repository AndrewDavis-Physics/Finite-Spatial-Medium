## Thought Experiment 12: The Baryonic Blow-Off and the Statistical Mass Gap

**The Scenario:** 
Following a core-collapse supernova, astronomical observations reveal a stark "Lower Mass Gap." The heaviest known neutron stars generally max out around 2.2 to 2.5 $M_\odot$, while the lightest known black holes begin around 5 $M_\odot$. The 3-to-4 $M_\odot$ weight class is statistically barren. Standard models struggle to explain this void. Relying on continuous mathematical limits (like the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff limit) that inevitably lead to unphysical singularities, mainstream physics assumes that matter entering this gap is physically crushed out of existence into an empty void. The Finite Spatial Medium (FSM) rejects this mathematical cliff. Because the continuous equations that produce the singularity are fundamentally broken, their associated breaking points are equally invalid. The FSM models the mass gap not as a structural prohibition of the spatial substrate, but strictly as the statistical artifact of bifurcated baryonic blow-off dynamics and the extreme rarity of secondary coalescences, operating across an unbroken continuum of compact mass objects.

**The Principal Architect's Core Mechanics Insight:**
> *"I think structurally when you have a supernova happen, it's either big enough to form a black hole or it blows too much off and forms a neutron star. But fundamentally, there's no physical divide between them; they are both compact mass objects sitting on a continuous sliding scale. As a compact mass object becomes more dense, an increasing percentage of its core turns into the saturated core of pure mass-energy clamped at the physical floor ($\text{ש}{MIN}$). It becomes a 'black hole' to an astronomer simply when it contracts inside its optical refraction threshold, but internally there's no functional cliff between an object with an 80% saturated core and one with 90%. The rarity of 3-to-4 solar mass objects isn't due to any spatial yield limit; it blows off too much mass in a standard supernova, and secondary collisions to build one up are exceptionally rare."*

**Substrate Architecture & The Compact Mass Continuum:** Under the framework's discrete mechanics, the spatial substrate responds to mass via localized volumetric node compression ($\text{ש}_V$). The spatial network possesses no arbitrary "yield strength" that chemically or structurally forbids a 3.5 $M_\odot$ mass anchor. The gap is driven by stellar ejection dynamics, not substrate limitations:
- **The Optical Curtain (The Saturated Scale):** Because the FSM dictates that compact mass objects clamp against a physical volumetric floor rather than collapsing into a zero-volume singularity, all compact bodies—from standard neutron stars up to supermassive black holes—occupy an unbroken physical continuum. What distinguishes a "black hole" is purely an optical threshold: when localized spatial coordinate compression steepens the surrounding gradient ($\text{ש}_V$) past the optical-kinematic refraction threshold (Postulate IX), outward-directed light is trapped, pulling down the Optical Curtain. Internally, there is no abrupt structural dividing line; as mass accumulates, the saturated core of pure mass-energy ($ש_V = \text{ש}_{MIN}$) simply claims a progressively larger percentage of the object's total physical volume. Whether a compact mass object in the transitional 3-to-5 $M_\odot$ bracket possesses an 80% or 95% saturated core remains an open empirical parameter, but the substrate accommodates the mass identically without requiring a singularity.
- **The Binary Blow-Off Extremes:** During a supernova, the collapsing core undergoes a violent elastomechanical ricochet. This massive kinetic rebound naturally bifurcates into two observational extremes: either the progenitor core is so overwhelmingly massive that the collapsing mass immediately drives the surrounding gradient past the optical refraction threshold, or the violent ricochet successfully blasts the vast majority of the envelope away. The exact final mass of a surviving visible neutron star is not fixed by an arbitrary mathematical cliff; it dynamically spans from roughly 1.2 to over 2.5 $M_\odot$ because the core's capacity to withstand collapse is strictly governed by its localized pre-existing spin rate (centrifugal shear support, $\text{ש}_S$) and the exact geometric asymmetry of the baryonic expulsion. There is no stable middle ground where a dying star cleanly halts its explosive ejection halfway.
- **The Merger Exception:** Because 3-to-4 $M_\odot$ objects are not physically forbidden by the spatial substrate, they can and do exist—but they must be built dynamically through secondary accumulation rather than born directly from isolated supernovae. For example, a 3.5 or 4 $M_\odot$ compact object can form if two neutron stars collide and merge, or if a dense compact object slowly siphons mass from a companion star. However, because these secondary macroscopic collisions are orders of magnitude less frequent than standard core-collapse supernovae, the resulting gap-mass objects are statistical outliers, leaving an apparent "gap" in standard stellar population surveys.

**Empirical Verification & Cosmological Diagnostics:**
If the Lower Mass Gap is a statistical artifact of stellar ejection and secondary merging rather than a fundamental physical breakdown, observational catalogs must reflect the presence of gap-mass objects exactly where secondary accretion or collisions occur.
- **Gravitational Wave Acoustic Scaling:** Current ground-based observatories (LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA) have begun detecting rare mergers involving objects in the mass gap (e.g., the 2.6 $M_\odot$ secondary object in GW190814). Standard astrophysics is highly divided on whether to classify these as the heaviest neutron stars or the lightest black holes. Next-generation broadband and space-based interferometers (such as LISA and the Einstein Telescope) will capture both the inspiral tidal deformability ($\Lambda$) and the ultra-low-frequency longitudinal volumetric compression pulses ($\Delta \text{ש}_V$) of these gap mergers. Telemetry will confirm that acoustic stiffness and tidal parameters scale continuously with mass, verifying that compact mass objects operate on an unbroken sliding scale of core saturation rather than jumping across a mathematical singularity cliff.
- **X-Ray Accretion "Fill-In":** The framework predicts that the 3-to-4 $M_\odot$ bracket will gradually populate not through new supernovae, but through High-Mass X-Ray Binaries (HMXBs). High-precision orbital telemetry from X-ray observatories will eventually confirm isolated cases of compact mass objects that began below the optical curtain but slowly siphoned enough mass from a companion over billions of years to creep upward into the 3.5 $M_\odot$ range. Whether this accreted mass eventually triggers the Optical Curtain or remains visible, the telemetry will prove the spatial substrate perfectly accommodates mass anchors inside the statistical gap.
