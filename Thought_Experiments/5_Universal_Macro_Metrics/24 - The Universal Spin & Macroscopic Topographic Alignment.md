**Substrate Architecture & The Universal Coriolis Effect:**
Under the model's discrete mechanics, the spatial network is not a static coordinate grid expanding symmetrically in all directions; it carries a macroscopic angular momentum vector inherited from the initial anisotropic rebound. The spatial substrate is rotating. This macro-scale rotation establishes a universal topological current, defining a global rotational axis, a universal equator, and two opposing kinematic hemispheres:
- **Topological Shear Bias:** As spiral galaxies coalesce and translate through this dynamic substrate, their internal angular momentum couples to the substrate's topological flow. Galaxies whose spin vectors align with the universal current encounter lower metric shear. Galaxies rotating against the current experience opposing metric shear and heightened structural strain across their perimeter nodes.
- **Global Bias vs. Local Turbulence:** The universal spin operates as a faint macro-bias, not an absolute dictator. In regions of extreme localized spatial compression—such as dense galaxy clusters and colliding filamentary intersections—local tidal torques completely overwhelm the background current. This ensures the universal spin manifests as a macro-scale statistical predominance (a 1% to 2% hemispheric excess) rather than a rigid 100/0 alignment.

**The Cosmic Doldrums (Equatorial Morphological Gradient):**
On planetary scales, the vertical component of the Coriolis force vanishes at the equator, preventing atmospheric updrafts from organizing into coherent rotational vortices. In the discrete spatial substrate, this creates an equatorial dead zone:
- **Suppression of Rotational Support:** Away from the equator, background topological vorticity provides a persistent low-energy bias that assists collapsing protogalactic clouds in settling into thin, rotation-supported disks ($V/\sigma \gg 1$, spiral galaxies).
- **Surge in Velocity Dispersion:** Along the universal equator, this background torque drops to zero. Without an external elastomechanical bias, collapsing baryonic gas and post-merger remnants are governed entirely by isotropic, random thermal collisions, favoring pressure-supported, three-dimensional stellar swarms ($V/\sigma \ll 1$, elliptical galaxies).
- **The Morphological Dead Zone:** The framework predicts a systematic cosmological gradient: the ratio of spiral galaxies to elliptical galaxies reaches a distinct minimum along the universal equatorial plane.

The seeming spatial disagreement between historical dipole axes—ranging from the high-latitude CMB multipoles ($b \approx +60^\circ$) to deep quasars ($b \approx +29^\circ$) and local galaxy clusters ($b \approx -20^\circ$)—is an artifact of treating the universe as a rigid, static snapshot rather than a time-dependent elastomechanical engine.

- **Lookback Time Vector Drift:** Redshift ($z$) serves as a direct core sample through the elastomechanical history of the substrate. The rotational axis is not a fixed coordinate, but a time-dependent vector $\mathbf{\hat{n}}(z)$. Because all probes share a common longitudinal corridor ($l \sim 210^\circ \text{ to } 240^\circ$) while sweeping progressively in latitude from $b \approx +60^\circ$ at the CMB down to $b \approx -20^\circ$ locally, the data directly records the physical precession arc of the universal rotation axis over 13.8 billion years.
